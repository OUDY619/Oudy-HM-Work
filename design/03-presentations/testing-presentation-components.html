LIBRARY 13 · CONTRIBUTING FACTOR · AUDIENCE STRATEGY

How ABO and CBO compete, cause damage, and help each other

Testing protects the measurement. Scaling maximizes delivery. Overlap determines whether they conflict.

Audience strategy
Stage
Strategy
Budget
Testing
ABO · cold audience targeting · one creative hypothesis per test cell
$10/day per creative
Scaling
Broad audience · CBO allocates campaign budget toward stronger delivery opportunities
Campaign-level budget
ABO testing system Controlled creative test Each $10 test needs enough independent delivery to judge the creative. Other ABO creatives can divide the same limited learning opportunity. Measurement role
Shared eligible usersCold targeting and broad delivery can overlap
CBO scaling system Broad delivery engine CBO shifts budget across ad sets toward opportunities with stronger expected value. It can reach people who also qualify for the ABO test. Growth role

Damage: testing loses an independent sample

The risk rises when the $10 ABO test, other ABO creatives, and the CBO can reach the same people during the same period.

Measurement risk
1ABO fragments its own signalParallel $10 creatives collect few events and can divide delivery across similar cold audiences.
2CBO reduces test opportunitiesWhen both campaigns qualify for a person, Meta advances one eligible ad set from the advertiser. The scaling system may advance more often.
3The next conversion gets harderThe test may reach high-propensity people first. Later impressions can carry lower expected conversion value or more repeated exposure.

Important nuance: the audience remains available, but easy conversions inside the test's current targeting, budget, and time window can become scarcer as exposure accumulates. Rising CPA alone does not prove that CBO consumed the audience.

SOP rule: Run ABO as a clean measurement cell. Promote a winner into CBO once, pause duplicate delivery, then use scaling results and exclusion audiences to shape the next test.
